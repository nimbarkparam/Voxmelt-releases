# VoxMelt Benchmark Results

## TL;DR

> **On a stock RTX 3080 Ti, VoxMelt's local Whisper large-v3 averaged 7.8% word error rate across five scripted real-world clips, held 3.1% on fast speech and on background noise, and transcribed 10 to 15 times faster than real time at a 4.2 GB VRAM peak. Audio bytes uploaded: zero.**
>
> **Added 2026-07-16: NVIDIA Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3 (int8 ONNX) on a plain CPU (Ryzen 9 5900X, GPU untouched) scored 11.9% WER on the same five clips at 11-13x realtime with a 4.4s model load - and beat large-v3-on-GPU outright on fast natural speech (2.0% vs 3.1%). Most of its overall gap is digit-formatting scorer penalties (CER 7.3% vs 6.1%), not mishearing. This is the real-recorded-speech number that upgrades the earlier clean-synthesized eval claim.**

## Accuracy (WER / CER)

Lower is better. Same normalization for every engine: lowercase, punctuation stripped, and for WER number words turned into digits one word at a time ("forty" to "40"), so "fifty eight" scores as "50 8" and an engine that writes "58" is charged two errors.

### VoxMelt large-v3 (local GPU)

| Clip | WER | CER | Ref words |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 clean dictation | 6.8% | 6.8% | 88 |
| 02 technical / code | 16.1% | 7.1% | 87 |
| 03 fast natural speech with filler | 3.1% | 2.3% | 98 |
| 04 medical jargon | 10.9% | 10.7% | 92 |
| 05 background noise | 3.1% | 4.2% | 96 |
| **Overall (word-weighted)** | **7.8%** | **6.1%** | **461** |

### VoxMelt medium (the 8 GB card option)

### VoxMelt Parakeet v3 (local CPU, no GPU) - added 2026-07-16

Run via `run_parakeet.py` using the shipping engine's `_ParakeetModel` (int8 ONNX,
CPUExecutionProvider, intra-op cap 6 threads, session-edge silence pads).
CPU: AMD Ryzen 9 5900X (24 logical cores). onnxruntime 1.24.4. Load: 4.4s.

| Clip | WER | CER | Ref words | Speed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 01 clean dictation | 8.0% | 7.2% | 88 | 12.1x realtime |
| 02 technical / code | 20.7% | 6.7% | 87 | 11.7x |
| 03 fast natural speech with filler | **2.0%** | **1.0%** | 98 | 12.9x |
| 04 medical jargon | 21.7% | 11.5% | 92 | 11.3x |
| 05 background noise | 8.3% | 10.3% | 96 | 12.1x |
| **Overall (word-weighted)** | **11.9%** | **7.3%** | **461** | **~12x, 0 VRAM** |

**Reading the Parakeet numbers honestly:**

- Clip 03 (fast natural speech with filler - the closest to real dictation) is
  the headline: 2.0% WER BEATS large-v3 on the 3080 Ti (3.1%).
- Clips 04/05 WER is heavily inflated by digit formatting: Parakeet writes
  "58", "$1800", "142 over 88", "21st" where the reference spells numbers out,
  and the strict scorer counts each as an error. The near-even CER (7.3% vs
  6.1% overall) shows the characters were mostly right. For dictation UX the
  digit output is arguably BETTER. Kept under the same under-claiming rule.
- Genuine misses: "atorvastatin" -> "at or vast 10", "lisinopril" split,
  "quote" -> "code" once. Rare-drug-name vocabulary is the real weak spot.
- The fair no-GPU comparison: whisper-medium scored 22.8% on these clips WITH
  a GPU. Parakeet's 11.9% with zero VRAM is the best no-GPU result we measured
  - that is the case for CPU-first as the default.
- Third-party context (different test set, not comparable): Open ASR
  Leaderboard (arXiv:2510.06961 Table 3) - Parakeet v3 6.32% avg WER / RTFx
  3332.7 vs Whisper large-v3 7.44% / RTFx 145.5.

**Reading the numbers honestly:**

- The code clip (02) is large-v3's weak spot at 16.1% WER, and we are leaving it in. Most of the gap is tokenization, not mishearing: the script says "useDebounce" as one token, the engine writes "use debounce" as two, and the scorer counts that as errors. The 7.1% CER on the same clip shows the characters are mostly right. Two genuine misses: "useDebalance" once, and "load-ash" for "lodash".
- The jargon clip (04) WER is inflated by the scorer in the engine's favor being IGNORED: the script spells out "fifty eight" and "forty milligrams"; the engine writes the better-formatted "58" and "40 mg" and gets penalized for it. We kept the stricter scoring. Drug names (metformin, lisinopril, atorvastatin, troponin) were all correct.
- Fast speech with filler (3.1%) and background noise (3.1%) are the standout results, and they are the clips that look most like real dictation.
- medium is 3x worse than large-v3 across the board. That is exactly why the app defaults to large-v3 and treats smaller models as a VRAM-constrained fallback, not a recommendation.

## The privacy claim (the part cloud tools cannot match)

While all ten runs above were transcribed, **audio bytes uploaded to any server: 0.** The founder's voice clips for this benchmark never left the machine, including for the scoring. VoxMelt's only network traffic is license checks, billing, and update checks, never audio, never transcripts. Verify it yourself: run any network monitor while you dictate.

## Caveats (left in on purpose, they build trust)

- Small sample: 5 clips, one speaker, English only.
- Single hardware configuration (above). Your numbers scale with your GPU.
- Founder-run, not a third-party lab. Reproduce it (below).
- No cloud engine column yet: running one means uploading the clips, and we defaulted to not doing that. The method supports it (`transcripts/NN.cloud-whisper.txt`), and the scorer treats any added engine identically.
- The scorer's number folding maps single words ("forty" to "40") but does not merge compounds ("fifty eight" to "58"), which penalizes the engine's digit formatting on clip 04. Kept as is: under-claiming beats over-claiming.

## Reproduce it

The clips' reference scripts, both runners and the scoring script are in this folder; the recordings and the engines' raw output are not. `python run_whisper_gpu.py` or `python run_parakeet_cpu.py`, then `python wer.py transcripts/`, runs the same method on your own clips and hardware.
